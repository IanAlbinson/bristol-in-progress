Bristol's public trees

What I'm working on

11 open files

Not everything the Town is doing. These are the files I'm following closely right now. Some are mine to move, some sit with staff or contractors, and some are board decisions I'm working toward.

Projects and infrastructure

Holley Hall energy retrofit

Open, punch list

A whole-building retrofit paid for by a $325,853.52 state grant through the Municipal Energy Resilience Program, with no local match required. The work covers the building envelope (door weatherstripping, window flashing, storm windows, air-seal sheathing and a vapor control layer, dense-pack wall insulation), HVAC (a heat pump, smart thermostat, heat recovery ventilation and ducting, and replacing the old radiation system), all-LED lighting throughout, and a heat pump water heater. The building was out of service for the work and has reopened, with a short punch list of items still to close out. The State's money has to be spent by the end of 2026. MERP grant 01155-A172-5443

Lawrence Memorial Library renovation

Subfloor going in

A $541,246.15 renovation paid for with federal Coronavirus Capital Projects Fund money passed through the Vermont Department of Libraries, with no local match ($483,000 base plus a $58,246.15 contingency). The work converts the old storage area into a soundproofed quiet room, installs a new propane heating system with an energy recovery ventilator, MERV 13 filters and ductwork, updates the electrical system, strips and replaces old insulation, repairs the roof and the building envelope, remediates lead paint, and adds an automatic door closer on the Children's Room tied to the fire alarm. Asbestos and PCB abatement came first. The new first-floor subfloor is going in now. Everything has to be complete by December 31, 2026. CPF grant 01130-2024-CPF-003

EV charging on the Town Green

Permits next

A Level 2 dual-port charger at the Park and North corner, replacing the existing bollard. The Selectboard approved it on August 24 at no cost to the Town, covered by a $2,400 Green Mountain Power incentive and a $20,561.94 grant. Rates are set at $0.25 per kWh plus a $2 session fee, and $1 an hour after four hours. The contract is signed. Zoning and right-of-way permits come next.

Town network and cameras

Phase 1 scoped

Network and camera work across Town buildings, pulled into one project in September. Phase 1 is ethernet runs at Holley Hall and the library for more reliable internet. Phase 2 covers cameras and recording, plus the Hub, Public Works and the Town Green, and waits on a camera policy the board has not adopted yet.

Downtown access and circulation study

Awaiting decision

A regional planning grant request to study parking, walking and traffic downtown. Pulls in the West Street work and a possible 25 mph speed reduction. Filed in August and pitched to the regional transportation committee on September 16. No decision back yet. See the downtown traffic map.

Town-wide police services

Board work session

Residents outside the village core pay for a police district they aren't in. The board is working through whether to put town-wide coverage to the voters, and in which year.

Main Street parking and sight lines

No owner yet

Full-size trucks parked on the Snap's block make Main Street hard to pass, and sight lines there are poor. Three remedies have been floated: signing those spaces for compact vehicles, prohibiting trucks, or re-angling the spaces east of the fire hydrant at the cost of one space. Raised at the September 14 roundtable with nobody assigned to it yet. Overlaps the downtown access and circulation study above.

Water and sewer

Bristol West water construction

In construction

Water main replacement in the west end, working block by block. Elm Street, Taylor Avenue, Maple Street and West Pleasant Street have all had work and notices. Shutoffs and flushing are posted to the Town website and Front Porch Forum as each block comes up, usually on short notice.

Bristol East water and the bond vote

Bond vote ahead

A water system project for the east side, in line for state loan forgiveness. Bristol scored 2nd among new projects statewide, and the State's adopted plan puts it at an estimated $4,333,841 loan with $2,166,921 of that forgiven. The State's schedule sets 90 percent design by December 1 and requires the bond vote to be warned by the end of January for a March ballot. Missing a deadline moves Bristol into the next funding cycle, so the ballot timing is largely set by the calendar rather than by preference.

Downtown sewer meeting

Being scheduled

A proposed public meeting between the Town and downtown business owners about the sewer system, with the Main Street water design likely folded in since the same businesses are affected either way. Date, format and scope are not settled.

Stalled

Fast EV charging

Waiting on interconnection

A DC fast charger for Bristol. Site and scope are still open, and it's waiting on utility interconnection. Nothing is moving on it right now.
