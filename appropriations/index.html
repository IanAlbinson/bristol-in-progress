Where Bristol's appropriations money goes

Every year Bristol voters approve a set of appropriations at Town Meeting: money that leaves the town budget and goes to a library, a rescue squad, a food shelf, a bus service. This page shows all thirty of them for FY2027, what each one does, and how much of the money stays in Bristol.

Please note

This is my own page, not an official Town of Bristol publication, and nothing here speaks for the Selectboard. The warned articles and the certified vote live with the Bristol Town Clerk. Financial figures come from each organization's own IRS filings, not from the Town.

The town library, first Article 11

Why it sits on its own. Lawrence Memorial Library is a municipal town library, a department of the Town rather than an independent nonprofit, and it files no IRS Form 990. At of every appropriation dollar it would flatten any chart it appeared in. Every figure below this point excludes it, so the comparison is nonprofit to nonprofit.

Bristol-based, and everyone else

Based in Bristol Regional or statewide

How this is counted. "Based in Bristol" means the organization's own address is in Bristol. It is not a measure of who benefits: Tri-Valley Transit runs buses through Bristol from a Middlebury office, and Bristol residents use the Counseling Service, HOPE and Age Well the same way their neighbors in Lincoln and Starksboro do. Regional organizations serve all of Addison County, so Bristol's contribution is a share of a much larger operation.

About these groupings. The categories are mine, not the Town's. The warned articles group recipients by Article 11 through 15, which is a budgeting structure rather than a description of the work. Each organization sits in exactly one category and the categories sum to the nonprofit total.

What this list is. Registered nonprofits in Bristol and the surrounding towns that receive no Town appropriation. It is here for context: the appropriations list is a small slice of the nonprofit activity in this area, and being left off it is the norm rather than the exception.

Reading the financial columns. These describe the size of each organization, not the Town's money. Figures are each org's most recent IRS Form 990 with a public data extract, mostly FY2024. Many file on a June or August fiscal year, so a newer return may exist but not yet be extracted. Small groups marked No 990 are usually 990-N postcard filers or operate under a parent organization or church and file no public financials.

Sources. Appropriation amounts and article numbers come from the Town of Bristol Annual Report, Articles 11 through 15, all adopted by voice vote at the March 2, 2026 Annual Town Meeting (FY2027). Financial figures come from ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer, which republishes IRS Form 990 data extracts. Asset breakdowns come from Part X of each organization's e-filed return. Population is the 2020 Census count for Bristol, 3,782.

How the numbers were checked. Every EIN was verified against ProPublica's organization endpoint. The Bristol and regional subtotals, the fourteen purpose categories and the by-town totals each reconcile to the appropriations total, with and without the library. Asset buckets are taken from the filed return and always sum to reported total assets.

Two cautions. "Addison County Restorative Justice Services" is the operating name of the registered entity Addison County Court Diversion & Community Justice Projects Inc. Officer compensation of $0 usually means an all-volunteer board, or that staff pay is reported on the salaries line instead, and it is not evidence that nobody is paid.

On the per-resident figure. It is total appropriations divided by population, nothing more. Appropriations are funded through the municipal property tax, so no resident pays that exact amount and what any given household pays depends on assessed value. It is offered as a sense of scale, not as a tax bill.
