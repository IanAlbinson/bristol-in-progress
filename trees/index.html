Sources

Tree records: the Municipal Tree Inventory layer of the Vermont Agency of Natural Resources Atlas, queried for the Town of Bristol on September 12, 2026. 668 records came back: 562 trees and 106 vacant planting sites. Species, diameter class, condition, consult flag, house number and coordinates are as the State holds them.

The inventory behind that layer: Bristol Public Tree Inventory Report, January 2015, prepared for the Town by the Vermont Urban & Community Forestry Program and the University of Vermont LANDS Field Semester. Field work was carried out by four LANDS students on September 16, 17 and 18, 2014, on 26 streets and 3 town-owned properties, using ArcGIS Collector on iPads. The report put annual benefits from these trees at $65,365 and existing canopy cover at 24.5% across the 0.58 square mile inventory area.

The 2026 revisit: my own walk, on foot, the evenings of September 11 and 12 2026. GPS-tagged phone photographs with typed field notes. Street addresses for those fourteen points are reverse-geocoded from the coordinates against OpenStreetMap, nearest addressed feature, not surveyed frontage.

Diversity targets: the 10-20-30 rule commonly used in urban forestry, that no single species should exceed 10% of the population, no genus 20%, and no family 30%.

How this was checked

Every count on this page is computed from the 668 records when the page loads. The condition totals and the top species counts computed here match the printed 2015 report line for line, which is the strongest evidence that the State layer and the report describe the same survey.

Genus and family are derived from the State's common-name species field using a fixed lookup, not guessed per record. The State's placeholder entries, "broadleaf deciduous medium" and the like, carry no genus and are excluded from the diversity shares rather than assigned one.

The 2026 matches are proposals, not links. Each one is the nearest 2014 tree to a 2026 pin, with the straight-line distance shown so you can judge it. A consumer phone GPS under a canopy is easily off by several metres, so a close match is a candidate to confirm in the field and nothing more. Where the second-nearest 2014 tree is almost as close, the match is marked ambiguous. Where the 2026 note names a species that conflicts with the 2014 record, or the geocoded address disagrees with the 2014 house number, that is flagged rather than reconciled.

Where the report and the State layer disagree

The report states that 44 trees were flagged for a future consultation. The State layer carries the consult flag on 68 records. I have not resolved which is right, and this page counts what the layer holds.

The report's Appendix A gives North Street 12 vacant planting spots; the layer has 22. That appendix is a scanned table run through OCR and its columns did not survive cleanly, so the layer is the better source, but neither is confirmed.

The report lists 25 green ash. The layer holds 23.

118 of the 668 records carry no street or site name in the layer. The report attributes trees to three town-owned properties, the Town Green, the Town Offices with the Historical Society, and the high school, but those attributions are not in the data and cannot be reconstructed from it, so this page groups them as site not recorded rather than assigning them a street.

The layer's photo fields point at an image for 632 records, but attachments are not enabled on the endpoint that serves this data, so none of those photographs are shown here.
