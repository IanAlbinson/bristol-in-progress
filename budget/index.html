The town budget, line by line

Bristol voters adopted a $4,134,345.73 town budget for FY2027 on March 2, 2026. This page shows where that money comes from, where it goes, what changed from last year, and every one of the 270 lines underneath.

Please note

This is my own page, not an official Town of Bristol publication, and nothing here speaks for the Selectboard. The adopted budget of record is the Town's own approved workbook and the certified Town Meeting minutes, both held by the Bristol Town Clerk. Where the printed Annual Report and the Town's workbook disagree, I have used the workbook and said so.

Where the money comes from FY2027 adopted

Almost nine dollars in ten is property tax. The Town collects some money on its own: recreation program fees, state highway aid, town clerk fees, current use payments, National Forest payments. That covers $450,780, about a ninth of the budget. Everything else is raised through the municipal property tax. The Fire Department and the voted appropriations have no outside revenue at all, so every dollar of both comes from taxes.

What changed, and why FY2026 adopted → FY2027 adopted

In the Selectboard's own words. At Town Meeting the drivers given for the general fund were personnel: salary increases, Planning and Zoning Administrator AZ Larsen and Grant Administrator Freeda Powers going from 32 hours to 40, and Community Resource Specialist Amy Smith's position moving fully onto the general fund as her ARPA funding ended. Health insurance fell, partly because employees moved onto spousal coverage. The single largest increase was the Capital Building and Maintenance Fund going to $120,000 from a typical $30,000, to support the Holley Hall and Lawrence Memorial Library projects and planning for a future public works building, with the Capital Sidewalk Fund cut to $20,000 to help pay for it. Public Works was driven by personnel, a contracted-services line covering roughly three years of deferred hazardous-tree work in one season, winter materials, and a new debt line for the Hewitt Road land bought for a future public works facility.

Reading the movers. These compare last year's adopted budget to this year's, line by line. A line that exists in only one of the two years is left out, so the two columns do not sum to the overall change.

What that has meant on a tax bill Municipal rates, FY2023 to FY2025

The FY2027 rate is not set here. The Selectboard sets the rate on the 2026 grand list after the budget is adopted, so the table below is the most recent three years the Town has published rather than a projection. A municipal rate is dollars per $100 of assessed value.

The town budget is the smaller half of a Bristol tax bill. In FY2025 the total municipal rate was $0.8281 while the state education homestead rate was $1.98, so roughly seven of every ten dollars on a homestead bill went to education, which the State sets and the Town does not control. Properties inside the Bristol Police District pay an additional district rate, $0.3263 in FY2025. The Common Level of Appraisal, which the State uses to adjust for how far assessments have drifted from market value, fell from 0.87 to 0.72 over these three years.

It is useful for spotting a line that was already over, and useless as a full-year comparison. No percentage on this page is calculated against it.

How the numbers were checked. All 270 line items were reconciled to their group subtotals, the 45 groups to their department totals, and the five departments to the summary: $4,134,345.73 in spending, $450,780.00 in non-tax revenue, $3,683,565.73 raised by taxes. Each department total was then checked against the motion adopted at Town Meeting, and all five match to the cent.

Two corrections to the printed Annual Report. On the Fire Department page the "Fire Department" group subtotal in the FY2026 actual column reads $17,793.89, but its own five lines add to $16,053.89, and that $1,740.00 carries into the printed department total of $182,796.95. The Town's workbook and the report's own summary page both give $181,056.95, which is the figure used here. Separately, the caption under the summary pie chart reads $3,683,566.73 where the table above it reads $3,683,565.73, a difference of one dollar; the table is right.

What is not on this page. This is the general fund, the part of the budget voters adopt at Town Meeting. The water and sewer funds are separate enterprise funds paid for by ratepayers rather than taxes, and the Bristol Police District is a separate district with its own grand list and its own rate. None of the three are included in any figure here.

On the per-resident figure. It is the tax-supported budget divided by population, nothing more. Municipal taxes are levied on assessed property value, so no resident pays that amount and what any household actually pays depends on what its property is worth. It is offered as a sense of scale, not as a tax bill.
