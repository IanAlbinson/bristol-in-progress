Bristol's sidewalks, point by point

In 2022 the Town had its sidewalks walked and scored, one short stretch at a time. It is the only condition record Bristol has, and it has lived inside a mapping app almost nobody opens.

Please note

This is my own page, not an official Town of Bristol publication, and nothing here speaks for the Selectboard. The inventory is the Town's, carried out and hosted by the Addison County Regional Planning Commission, whose map remains the source of record. It is a planning document, not an engineering or ADA survey, and it is four years old.

The mapClick any point for its full record

Photo markers open the surveyor's original image on ACRPC's server. They are unresized phone photographs averaging 5.3 MB, so nothing loads until you ask for one.

What was measuredEvery point scored on all four, plus surface weathering

These four are the reliable part of the survey. All 1,399 points carry a score on every one, drawn from a fixed list, so they can be counted without interpretation. Read a "None" as nothing recorded on that dimension, not as a certificate.

About that overall ratingWhy this page leads with the four defects

The survey also carries a single 0-to-5 score, and it does not behave like a grade. Its legend reads 0 = best, 5 = worst. But 963 points are scored 0, and 272 of those carry a Medium or Severe defect. Ratings 1 through 5 are all between 74% and 87% severe, so the ladder between them separates very little. What it reliably tells you is 0 versus not-0.

What the surveyors wrote

One note is withheld. It records a driveway transition needing regrading and names the property and its owner, who had offered to help with the work. It is in the Town's copy of the survey; it does not need to be on a web page.

Source. The Town of Bristol's 2022 sidewalk inventory, conducted and hosted by the Addison County Regional Planning Commission. Read live from ACRPC's public feature services on September 12, 2026: survey layer survey123_55f17033edbb4f5bb88bc7fa884f9aff and photo layer Bristol_Sidewalk_survey_PHOTOS.

Why the count is lower than ACRPC's. The survey layer returns 1,644 records. 245 are exact duplicates, identical on every field with object IDs offset by exactly 81, the signature of one block uploaded four times. This page counts the 1,399 distinct points; ACRPC's map displays all 1,644, so its totals run about 15% high. The photo layer has no duplicates.

How the fields were read. The form was revised partway through collection, so several measures exist under two or three column names. In every case the older columns are empty on all records and exactly one is populated on all of them, so nothing was merged or inferred. Two fields label the middle severity "Medium" and two label it "Moderate"; both appear here as one step. "Sidewalk Width" is a coded 0-to-5 value with no documented meaning and is not shown.

What is left out. The survey records who collected each point; that is not reproduced here. Neither is the note described above. There is no linear sidewalk layer, only points, so nothing here can be stated as a share of Bristol's sidewalk network, only as a share of points surveyed. Every record timestamps to April 2023 though the inventory is titled 2022.
