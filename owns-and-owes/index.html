What the Town owns and owes

Past the annual budget there is a balance sheet: $4.79 million of debt running out to 2059, $2.22 million sitting in reserve funds, 161.87 acres of town property, and $42.7 million of land in Bristol that pays no property tax at all.

Please note

This is my own page, not an official Town of Bristol publication, and nothing here speaks for the Selectboard. Every figure is transcribed from the 2025 Annual Report, pages 37 through 43. Where the report's own rows and totals disagree I have said so rather than quietly picking one, and those cases are listed at the bottom.

What the Town owes Bonds and notes payable

Governmental, paid by taxes Business-type, paid by water and sewer ratepayers

Two kinds of debt. Governmental obligations are repaid out of the tax-supported budget, and their payments are the debt service lines you can see in the town and district budgets. Business-type obligations belong to the water and sewer funds and are repaid by ratepayers, not taxpayers. The Fire Station bond is the largest single balance and runs to 2046; the USDA West Street water bond runs to 2059, thirty-three years from now.

What the Town holds Capital and dedicated reserve funds, FY2025

Two funds ended the year negative. The Capital Technology Fund closed at −$712.42 and the Cemetery Reserve Fund at −$11,469.15, the latter because $11,475.00 was spent against a fund that began the year holding $5.85. The Capital Paving Fund spent $285,302.75 against a $135,000.00 appropriation and ended at $27,442.46, down from $177,745.21. Three dedicated funds are restricted: the Martha Parker and A. Johnson principals cannot be spent, and the Peveril Peake fund is limited to work on Holley Hall.

What the Town owns

Values are listed values, not market prices. These are the assessed figures the listers carry for town-owned property, which is exempt from taxation anyway, so they are a bookkeeping record rather than an appraisal. The only change between 2024 and 2025 is Hewitt Road, the 4.7 acre parcel bought in June 2024 for a future public works facility, appearing at $16,500.

What pays no property tax Non-taxable property in Bristol, 2025

$42.7 million off the rolls. Schools are four fifths of it, and Mt. Abraham Unified School District alone is $24.9 million. For scale, Bristol's FY2025 municipal grand list was $3,618,822, which represents roughly $362 million of taxable value, so non-taxable property is equivalent to about a ninth of the taxable base. That is a rough comparison between two differently compiled figures, not an official ratio, and none of this property could be taxed in any case. Town-owned property in the section above is exempt too and is not counted here.

Rolling stock

Ages are model years, not purchase dates. The Annual Report lists a model year for each vehicle, and two of them, both 1993 Fords, are the oldest apparatus the Fire Department runs. The capital vehicle and equipment funds in the section above exist to replace this list on a schedule; the Fire Vehicle Fund held $488,531.26 at the end of FY2025.

Sources. Every figure is from the Town of Bristol 2025 Annual Report: Bonds and Notes Payable and the audit note, page 37; Town & District Capital Funds Report, page 38; Town of Bristol Buildings and Land, page 39; Value of Non-Taxable Properties, page 40; Equipment List, page 41; Delinquent Tax Report, pages 42 and 43. The FY2025 municipal grand list figure used for the scale comparison is from the Three Year Comparison sheet of the Town's approved FY2027 budget workbook. Audits for the fiscal years ending June 30, 2023 and 2024 were completed in 2025 by RHR Smith & Company and are posted at bristolvt.org/documents-and-forms.

How the numbers were checked. Every row was added back to its printed group total and every group total to its printed grand total. The bond schedule reconciles exactly in all four money columns on both sides. Seven places did not reconcile, and rather than silently correcting them this page shows the printed figures and lists the gaps above.

What is not here. This is a balance-sheet snapshot as printed, not an audit and not a full financial statement. The audited statements are the authoritative record. Fund balances are as of the close of FY2025 and have moved since. Debt outstanding is as printed in the report, not as of today.
